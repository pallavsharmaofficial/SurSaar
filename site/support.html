The app stays free either way

SurSaar has no advertising, no subscription and nothing to buy inside it. It does not collect your data, so there is nothing to sell about you either. That is deliberate, and it is how it will stay.

It does take time. The pitch and chord detection runs on your own device and had to be made accurate enough to trust, the song catalogue is curated rather than scraped, and every lesson is written out by hand. If the app has helped you play something you could not play before, you can send something towards that.

Other ways that cost nothing

  • Tell someone. A person who would use a free, honest kundli app almost certainly does not know this one exists.
  • Rate it on Play. Ratings are what decide whether Play shows an app to anyone at all.
  • Report what is wrong. A chord it misheard, or a song whose timing is off, is worth knowing about. Open an issue.
  • Improve it. The source is public and MIT licensed.
